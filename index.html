Remedial Massage.

Assessment-led treatment built around what is actually causing the pain.

What It Involves

Every remedial session starts with a short assessment of posture, movement and problem areas, so the treatment is built around what is actually causing the pain, not just where it is felt. From there, a combination of techniques is used, which might include trigger point work, stretching and deep tissue massage, depending on what your body needs.

Benefits

  • Targets the cause: treats the underlying issue rather than just the sore spot.
  • Tailored technique: combines whichever methods suit your body and condition.
  • Take-home exercises: often paired with stretches to help recovery between sessions.
  • Health fund & WorkCover: registered provider with most major health funds and WorkCover.
  • Ongoing plans: sessions build on each other rather than starting from scratch each time.

Good For

Chronic pain, sports or work-related injuries, and anyone wanting a structured, ongoing treatment plan rather than a one-off session.

Deep Tissue Massage.

Firm, focused pressure for the muscle layers a light massage cannot reach.

What It Involves

Deep tissue massage uses slow strokes and sustained pressure to work through the deeper layers of muscle and connective tissue. It targets adhesions and chronic tension, often through the neck, shoulders, lower back and legs, with pressure that builds gradually over the session. It is more intense than a relaxation massage, and communicating your comfort level as you go is part of getting it right.

Benefits

  • Breaks down adhesions: works into the deeper muscle layers where tight, fibrous "knots" build up.
  • Eases chronic tension: targets ongoing tightness through the neck, shoulders and lower back.
  • Improves range of motion: loosens tissue that is been restricting how a joint or muscle group moves.
  • Supports recovery: helpful alongside rehab from repetitive strain or overuse.
  • Pairs with remedial work: often combined with trigger point therapy or cupping in the same session.

Good For

People carrying tension from desk work or manual labour, athletes managing tight muscle groups, and anyone who has found a lighter massage does not quite reach the source of their pain.

Relaxation Massage.

Slower, lighter strokes to ease everyday stress and tension.

What It Involves

Relaxation massage uses broad, flowing strokes at a gentler pressure than remedial or deep tissue work. Rather than treating a specific injury, it is aimed at winding down: easing the everyday tension that builds up from work, stress or simply not having had a moment to slow down.

Benefits

  • Reduces everyday stress: a lower-pressure session to help you switch off.
  • Eases general muscle tension: without the intensity of deep tissue work.
  • Supports better sleep: a calming way to end a stressful week.
  • Good starting point: a gentle introduction if you have not had a massage before.

Good For

General wellbeing and stress relief, or anyone who would prefer a calmer, lower-pressure session over a firm one.

Pregnancy Massage.

Safe, supportive massage adapted for each stage of pregnancy.

What It Involves

Pregnancy massage adapts positioning and pressure to suit each stage of pregnancy, often side-lying with supportive cushioning, and avoids areas or techniques that are not appropriate during pregnancy. The focus is on easing the discomfort that comes with a changing body: lower back pain, hip tension and swelling in the legs and feet.

Benefits

  • Eases back & hip discomfort: addresses the tension that builds as posture shifts.
  • Helps with swelling: pairs well with lymphatic drainage for the legs and feet.
  • Supports better sleep: a calming session for a body that is working hard.
  • Adapted positioning: safe, comfortable setup at every stage of pregnancy.

Good For

Expecting mothers experiencing the aches and discomfort that come with pregnancy.

Please check with your GP, obstetrician or midwife before your first pregnancy massage, particularly in the first trimester.

Cupping.

Suction-based therapy that lifts fascia, eases tension and improves circulation.

What It Involves

Cupping uses silicone cups placed on the skin to create gentle suction, lifting the skin and fascia away from the tissue underneath, the opposite of the pressure used in massage. Cups may be left in place or moved slowly across the area. It can leave temporary circular marks that fade within a few days.

Benefits

  • Eases fascial tightness: the lifting action releases restrictions massage pressure cannot reach.
  • Improves circulation: increased blood flow can support muscle recovery.
  • Recovery aid: a common addition for athletes managing training load.
  • Complements deep tissue work: often used together in the same session.

Good For

Athletes managing muscle recovery, people with fascial tightness, and clients curious about trying something a little different.

Lymphatic Drainage.

Gentle, rhythmic strokes that support the body's natural drainage system.

What It Involves

Lymphatic drainage uses very light, rhythmic pumping strokes that follow the direction of natural lymph flow. Rather than working into muscle tissue, it encourages fluid movement through the lymphatic system. The pressure used is much lighter than a typical massage, and the session as a whole is slower and more gentle.

Benefits

  • Reduces swelling: encourages movement of fluid that is pooled or built up in the tissue.
  • Supports recovery: a gentle option after surgery or injury, once cleared by your doctor.
  • Eases sinus congestion: light facial and neck work can help with pressure and stuffiness.
  • Complements pregnancy massage: often used alongside it for swelling in the legs and feet.
  • Calming session: the light pressure makes it a restorative, low-intensity treatment.

Good For

Clients recovering from surgery or injury, people managing swelling or fluid retention, and anyone wanting a gentle, restorative session rather than a firm one.

If you are recovering from surgery, please check with your doctor before booking a lymphatic drainage session.

Home / Services / Trigger point therapy

Trigger Point Therapy.

Direct pressure on the exact points causing your referred pain.

What It Involves

Trigger point therapy applies focused, sustained pressure to specific tight bands of muscle, trigger points, that refer pain to other parts of the body. A point in the shoulder, for example, can be the real cause of a recurring headache. Pressure is held for several seconds at a time until the muscle starts to release.

Benefits

  • Relieves referred pain: addresses pain that shows up somewhere other than its source.
  • Helps with headaches: neck and shoulder trigger points are a common headache cause.
  • Eases jaw tension: a targeted option for tightness through the jaw and temples.
  • Combines well: often layered into a remedial or deep tissue session.

Good For

Headaches, jaw tension, or pain that seems to travel to a different part of the body than where the problem actually is.

Home / Services / Acupressure

Acupressure.

Targeted pressure on specific points of the body to relieve tension and restore movement.

What It Involves

Acupressure uses sustained, targeted pressure on specific points of the body, applied with the fingers, thumbs or palms. No needles are used. It is often combined with other massage techniques in the same session, and the pressure is adjusted to what feels comfortable for you.

Benefits

  • Eases tension: focused pressure on tight areas helps muscles let go.
  • Supports movement: releasing stiff areas can help restore comfortable range of motion.
  • Needle-free: a hands-on option for people who prefer to avoid needles.
  • Complements remedial work: often used alongside other techniques in the same session.

Good For

People with muscle tension, stiffness or restricted movement, and clients who prefer a gentle, needle-free, hands-on approach.

How we collect, use, store and protect your personal and health information.

Last updated: 10 October 2026

iCare Remedial Massage ("we", "us") provides remedial, medical, sports and relaxation massage in Mulgrave, Victoria, with home visits across Melbourne's south east. This policy explains how we collect, use, store and share your personal and health information, and how you can access or correct it.

Storage and Overseas Providers

We keep your information in secure electronic or paper form, limit access to the people who need it, and use passwords and other reasonable safeguards.

Cancellation Policy.

What we ask when you need to cancel or change an appointment.

Last updated: 10 October 2026

We understand that life happens and plans change! To ensure we can accommodate other clients, we ask for a minimum of 24 hours' notice if you need to cancel or change your appointment. Failure to provide 24 hours' notice may result in a cancellation fee of up to 50% of your treatment cost.

How to Cancel

If you need to cancel or change your appointment, please contact us by email at Icaremassage24@proton.me or by calling us on 0455 519 408. Please include your name and the date and time of your appointment so we can find your booking.

How the 24 Hours' Notice Works

  • Notice must reach us at least 24 hours before your appointment start time. For example, for a 2:00 pm appointment on Friday, we need to hear from you by 2:00 pm on Thursday.
  • Notice must be given by email to Icaremassage24@proton.me or by calling 0455 519 408. Please include your name and the date and time of your appointment so we can find your booking.
  • Your notice counts from the time we receive your email or answer your call, not from the time you wrote it. We will reply to confirm that your cancellation or change has been received. If you do not hear back, please follow up so there is no confusion.
  • The 24 hours is counted in real time, including weekends and public holidays, not business hours.
  • If you would like to reschedule rather than cancel, include a few times that suit you. Changing your appointment to a new time also needs 24 hours' notice.

No-Shows

If you do not attend your appointment and have not contacted us, we may charge a cancellation fee of up to 50% of your treatment cost.

Emergencies and Illness

We know that genuine emergencies and sudden illness happen. Please contact us as soon as you can, and we will consider your circumstances. We may waive the fee at our discretion.

Home Visits

The same 24 hours' notice applies to home and workplace visits. Because our therapist sets aside time for travel as well as treatment, short-notice cancellations of home visits cannot be filled by another client.

Funded Sessions

Cancellation fees generally cannot be claimed from a health fund. They may also not be claimable under WorkCover or a Home Care Package. Where that is the case, the fee is payable by you directly, or as set out in your service agreement.

If We Need to Cancel

If we have to cancel or change your appointment, we will let you know as early as we can and offer you another time. You will not be charged for an appointment we cancel.

Funding and Claims.

How health fund, WorkCover and Home Care Package claims work with us.

Last updated: 10 October 2026

We are a registered provider with most major health funds and a WorkCover registered provider, and we are an approved Home Care Package provider. This page explains how claims work, so there are no surprises.

Private Health Funds

  • Your rebate depends on your fund, your level of cover, your annual limits and any waiting periods. Please check your cover with your fund before you book.
  • You pay for your appointment on the day, and any rebate is paid to you by your fund according to your policy.
  • We are not responsible if your fund reduces or declines a claim.

WorkCover

  • To treat you under a WorkCover claim, we need your accepted claim number, your claim agent's details, and the agent's approval for the treatment before your appointment.
  • If treatment has not been approved, or the agent does not pay all or part of our fee, the unpaid amount is payable by you unless we have agreed otherwise in writing.
  • We may share your treatment information with your claim agent as needed to process your claim, in line with our Privacy Policy.

Home Care Packages

  • We need a service agreement and approved budget agreed with you and your package manager or care coordinator before we start.
  • Session and travel fees are as shown on our Pricing page, including the Allied Health Assistant rate where it applies to funded sessions.
  • If your package funds do not cover a session or a fee, the gap is payable by you, or sessions may need to pause until funding is available.

Cancellation Fees

Cancellation fees may not be claimable from your health fund, WorkCover or your package. See our Cancellation Policy.

Receipts and Information

Please give us accurate details for your claim, and keep your receipts. If you are not sure whether your funding is accepted, email Icaremassage24@proton.me or call 0455 519 408 before you book and we will help.
